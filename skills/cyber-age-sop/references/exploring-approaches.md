# Exploring Project Approaches

Read when the owner wants project brainstorming, or their purpose is reasonably clear but the way to meet it is still open. Use it to help them see and correct meaningful alternatives, then converge. It is not a mandatory stage for a settled plan, routine implementation or a clear small edit.

## Start with Their Situation

Read the supplied context and applicable earlier decisions. Preserve the owner's words, confirmed constraints and rejected suggestions. If the purpose is still unclear, continue Discovery with the most useful open question; do not replace listening with an immediate menu.

When a question or change of angle needs explanation, give one concrete reason: what it could change for the user. Ask only about a consequential gap. Do not require the owner to choose a brainstorming technique or learn technical vocabulary.

## Generate, Then Compare

- When the approach is open, try a useful lens: how the person starts and completes the work, how information is organized, what can be removed, or how an interaction could work differently. Select for the project; do not enumerate a technique catalog.
- Let the owner finish an idea before evaluating it. Treat new suggestions as provisional, distinct from their stated need. Research may clarify facts, but market convention alone does not invalidate a new idea.
- Generate plausible paths before narrowing them against the actual constraints. Show only a few useful survivors; they must differ in workflow, structure or a material tradeoff, not just names or styling adjectives. One reasonable path is sufficient when alternatives would be artificial. Do not target an idea count.
- Compare what the person would do or see, the benefit, the burden and the important uncertainty. Recommend a path when there is enough basis, explain why and what could change that advice. Leave it conditional when the missing information matters.
- Use `web-research.md` if a decision depends on external evidence. Use `technical-translation.md` when the tradeoff is technical. Load only the relevant reference; brainstorming does not require another Skill, search service or model.

## Make the Difference Visible

Use a small table, plain-text wireframe or flow when it helps distinguish the paths. Follow `visual-aids.md` for richer views. Show the essentials in the conversation, label the shape provisional and invite corrections. A useful shape describes an actual interaction or information structure; it is not a decorative illustration.

Illustrative interaction contrast, not a recommendation for every app:

```text
Capture-first: open → enter the item immediately → organize it later
Browse-first:  open → see existing items → choose where to add one
```

The difference is what happens on opening and when organizing work happens. If the user needs immediate capture, that may favor the first path; if choosing the right existing place matters more, the second may fit. Explain consequences in their terms rather than asking for a framework preference.

## Converge without Reopening Settled Work

Read back the chosen direction, relevant first-version boundary and remaining uncertainty. Reuse applicable substantive confirmation; otherwise let the owner correct or confirm before the first execution Card. An Agent recommendation, a preferred sketch or silence is not owner adoption. A request to discuss alternatives is not authorization to build them.

Once a direction is settled, continue the authorized work. Reopen only the part affected by a new requirement, conflict or evidence; preserve unrelated decisions. Record adopted changes in the existing Brief when record maintenance is authorized. Rejected proposals must not reappear as requirements in prompts, tasks or visuals.

A successful round makes the choice understandable and correctable. A longer conversation, more ideas or a rendered view alone does not prove that the owner understands or has learned a reusable principle.
